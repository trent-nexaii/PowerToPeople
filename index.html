About the book

About the book

In the early 1980s, New Zealand enjoyed some of the cheapest power prices in the world — the product of hydro-electricity schemes designed and constructed by the Ministry of Works.

For over 40 years, this government department overcame complex geological conditions, staff shortages and a constantly shifting political climate to develop a technically sophisticated, largely renewable electricity infrastructure network that produced more than $30 million dollars in annual revenue upon completion and continues to generate 80% of New Zealand's electricity supply.

It was a remarkable achievement for a government department that, since it was abolished in 1988, has been portrayed by its detractors as bloated and inefficient.

For the first time, Power to the People explores how this agency responded successfully to a post-war electricity crisis, concluding with construction of the Tongariro Power Development and Rangipo underground power station. Through oral histories and extensive research, it captures the sense of mission felt by the men and women who made the department what it was.

From this emerges a complex and human narrative that defies the ideological simplicities that ultimately engulfed the Ministry of Works and provides a timely reevaluation of a vital government agency, as New Zealand again faces an urgent infrastructure crisis – this time exacerbated by the effects of climate change.

About the author

Gillian Tompsett

Gillian Tompsett is a historian currently undertaking research for a PhD on the Ministry of Works. Her father was the Deputy City Engineer for the Napier City Council during the 1970s and she has worked on construction sites as a labourer.

She is interested in recording the oral histories of the people who helped build New Zealand's post-war public works infrastructure and lives with her family in Wellington.
