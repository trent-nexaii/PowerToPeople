About the book

The book dispels anti-public sector myths about large national infrastructure provision and argues that for nearly a century, the Ministry of Works and Development exemplified a mix of cutting-edge technology, sufficient funding, long-term institutional knowledge, skilled people, and a collective commitment to the public purpose of nation building.

A timely reminder of New Zealand's history of exceptional capability in environmentally-friendly engineering, and of the dangers ahead if this expertise continues to languish.

About the author

Gillian Tompsett is a historian currently undertaking research for a PhD on the Ministry of Works. Her father was the Deputy City Engineer for the Napier City Council during the 1970s and she has worked on construction sites as a labourer.

She is interested in recording the oral histories of the people who helped build New Zealand's post-war public works infrastructure and lives with her family in Wellington.
