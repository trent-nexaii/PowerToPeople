The Ministry of Works and Construction of the Tongariro Power Development

A massive project in the 1960s and 70s.

A nation at the far end of the earth, distant from all resources.

A public service enterprise of the first order.

For nearly a century the Ministry of Works and Development held together cutting-edge technology, sufficient funding, long-term institutional knowledge, skilled people, and a collective commitment to the public purpose of nation building.

This book sets that record against the myths about what the public sector can build — and asks what is lost when the expertise is allowed to languish.

A timely reminder not only of the country's history of exceptional capability in environmentally-friendly engineering but of the dangers ahead if this expertise continues to languish because of political ideology.

Diane BrandDirector of Academic Reviews, Office of the Vice-Chancellor, Waipapa Taumata Rau, University of Auckland

This book has rekindled a lot of bitter-sweet memories and assuaged a long simmering injustice and indifference. We feel a renewed pride in what was achieved but barely recognised at the time.

Gary GayResident Engineer, Ministry of Works and Development

Gillian Tompsett

A historian researching a PhD on the Ministry of Works. Her father was Deputy City Engineer for Napier City Council through the 1970s, and she has worked on construction sites as a labourer. She records the oral histories of the people who built New Zealand's post-war public works, and lives with her family in Wellington.
